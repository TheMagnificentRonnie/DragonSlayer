> What it does

▐▌▐▌▐▌

Live view over USB

Sees exactly what the camera sees. Any body libgphoto2 supports.

◐ ◑ ◒

Onion skinning

Full-frame ghost or edge outlines of the previous frame. Cyan tint. Adjustable.

▶ ⏸ ⏭

Two clean modes

CAPTURE for shooting, PREVIEW for reviewing. Space captures. Arrows scrub.

▓▒░

Scenes, ordered

Drag to reorder, rename inline, per-scene fps. IDs like sc010 so you can insert sc015.

▲▽▲▽

Never lose a frame

Every capture is a filesystem transaction. Journal-based recovery. Crash and reboot? Fine.

▚▚▚

MP4 & ProRes

H.264 for playing anywhere, ProRes 422 for editing. Source / 4K / 1080p, crop or fit.

f/ ISO

Camera settings

Aperture, shutter, ISO, white balance, RAW + JPEG. Set them from the app, never touch the camera mid-shot.

◷ ◶ ◵

Interval capture

N frames, S seconds apart. Time-lapses of a set build or a slumping puppet.

▓ ▓ ▓

Dockable panels

Scenes, Viewer, Timeline, Camera, Exposure, Onion, Export. Grab a tab, drop it anywhere.

⚙ ⚙ ⚙

Windows drivers included

Bundles Zadig for a one-click WinUSB swap. In-app button, no rabbit hole.

[✓][✗]

Camera diagnosis

Checks the USB port's driver, hubs, and whether the camera answers. Tells you the fix. Spots a port that needs Zadig by itself.

▣ ▣ ▣

Mac app, batteries in

Download, drag to Applications, shoot. Camera drivers and ffmpeg ride inside. Apple Silicon.

◉ ◎ ◉

Three themes

Dark teal (default), dark amber, or light. Preferences → Theme. Swap live.

> Cameras that work

libgphoto2 covers hundreds of DSLR and mirrorless bodies made in the last 15 years. Most Canon EOS, Nikon, Sony Alpha, Panasonic Lumix, Fujifilm and OM System / Olympus.

  • Everything else should work — open an issue with your model and results.

> Get started · Windows

  1. Download dragonslayer-*-windows-x64.zip from the latest release. Unzip anywhere.
  2. Plug in your camera on USB. Turn it on. Set the USB mode to PC, PC(Tether) or PTP.
  3. Run DragonSlayer.cmd.
  4. If Windows still owns the camera driver, click Set up USB driver… top-right. The bundled Zadig does the one-time WinUSB swap. Nothing on the camera itself changes. The swap belongs to that USB port: keep using the same one, straight into the computer, not a hub.
  5. Mode dial on M, click New project, press Space to capture. Hit H for in-app help. Camera acting up? Help → Diagnose camera…

> Get started · macOS

  1. Download dragonslayer-…-macos-arm64.zip from the Releases page. Apple Silicon (M1 or newer), macOS 12+.
  2. Unzip it and drag DragonSlayer.app into Applications.
  3. First launch only: macOS blocks unsigned apps once. Double-click, click Done, then System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway. (Or: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/DragonSlayer.app)
  4. Plug in your camera in PC / PC(Tether) / PTP mode. Quit Photos and Image Capture if they pop up.
  5. Click New project, Space to capture, H for help.

Rather build it yourself? The macOS setup guide starts from zero.

> Project folder

A project is a plain folder. Copy it to a USB stick. Hand it in.

MyFilm/
  project.json                  # name, fps, scene order
  scenes/
    sc010/
      scene.json
      journal.ndjson            # capture history
      frames/
        000001.jpg
        000001.CR2
      incoming/                 # in-flight; recovered after a crash
      trash/                    # deleted frames (never destroyed)
  trash/                        # deleted scenes
  exports/                      # compiled videos

> Why this exists · support the project

DragonSlayer was built for my teenage daughter.

She has a Panasonic Lumix GH5 — a proper camera, saved up for over ages — and a head full of stop-motion ideas. The software that would let her tether that camera to her computer costs more than the camera did. So I built this instead.

It's what she shoots with now. It's what her friends at school are starting to use. It'll always be free.

If it saves your kid, your class, your club, or you personally the price of a commercial licence, and you can spare a coffee, it means a real lot. Everything raised on Ko-fi goes back into the project — SD cards, spare cameras to test compatibility, the odd late-night pizza while I chase down a Panasonic PTP quirk.

Nothing here comes out of a company. It's one dad and a laptop. If you can't tip, use it anyway. That's the whole point.

> What we won't build

  • No AI features. No auto-onion, no in-betweening, no generative anything. Every frame is a person's choice. Hard line.
  • No video editing. Export to a real editor (see the list above).
  • No vector animation, tweening or drawing tools.
  • No 3D rigging or puppeting.
  • No paid tiers, subscriptions, telemetry, or accounts. Ever.

> A passion project · the small print

DragonSlayer is a passion project. I love animation, and I'm a senior software engineer, so I build this with the same care I'd put into professional work: every capture is written safely to disk, crashes are recovered on the next launch, and it's tested on real cameras before each release. My aim is simple: nobody should lose out, whether that's a frame, a film, or the money for expensive software.

As with all open-source software, it comes with no warranty (the MIT licence has the formal wording). Keep your camera card until a film is finished, and back up your project folders.

If something goes wrong, let me know and I'll do my absolute best to help. Just bear in mind I have a full-time job, so replies come in evenings and weekends rather than straight away.
